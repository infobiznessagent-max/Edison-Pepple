Premium
Guest Club

A full-service hotel in Nigeria was offering far more than its rooms suggested.
Almost none of that goodwill carried forward.

Guests rated their stays well, yet every visit was anonymous, every return booking came back through a booking app, and every stay started from zero.

Over twelve months, I led the research, design and rollout of Premium Guest Club alongside the hotel's management and frontline teams. This case study follows three of those guests from the moment they booked to the moment they came back.

02

Context

The hotel already had what guests wanted; it lacked a way to know them.

The hotel offered a pool, spa, gym and event hall, plus seasonal cultural nights that drew corporate travellers, long-stay engineers, December homecoming families and local residents alike. Its amenities competed with the best in the city. Its memory did not.

The city

Port Harcourt is the centre of Nigeria's oil and gas industry. Engineers arrive on rotation for weeks at a time, booked by corporate travel desks. In December, families return home for the holidays. Year-round, locals look for a place to train, unwind and be seen.

05 Below the line

What has to be true backstage for recognition to work.

Recognition at the front desk requires operational discipline behind it. These are the backstage systems, standards and fail-safes that made memory consistent across shifts.

Four backstage systems

The member record

One shared profile: preferences, stay notes, tier and consent. Built on the club platform.

The daily rhythm

Arrival list at 21:00, briefings at shift handover, a night-7 long-stay flag, and charges posted by close of day.

Infrastructure standards

Network on backup power, weekly switchover tests, and devices registered before arrival.

Contact and consent rules

Consent given at joining, at most one message a month, opt-outs tracked, and member data never sold.

07 · The club

A membership the hotel could run, and guests wanted to earn

Premium Guest Club put the five principles into practice: earned status, small circles, and a private lab for new ideas.

The house rule

Margin before volume. Recognition before discount.

No standing room discount, ever. Members got recognition, access and priority. A member price appeared only inside a specific product, set on that pilot's sign-off sheet.

Three ways to earn status

Stay

Nights and repeat visits

Spend

Rooms, restaurant, bar, spa, gym

Contribution

Referring, feedback, hosting, testing

A member who referred two corporate accounts and hosted a circle evening could reach Premium without outspending a frequent guest. Thresholds were set from the hotel's own spend data.

Three tiers, each one earned

Standard

Every guest, after a first stay

Recognition known by name, every outlet
Access member events, one circle
Say votes on ideas

Premium

Regulars who stay, spend and take part

Recognition preferences remembered
Access priority booking, two circles
Say tests new offers first

Gold

By points or invitation

The club's most committed members

Recognition the GM as direct contact
Access all four circles, first call on the gala
Say a seat on the member panel

Each tier includes everything in the one before. Inactive for two quarters? Reported as lapsed, and Blessing reached out.
